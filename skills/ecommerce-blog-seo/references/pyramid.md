# The organic visibility pyramid

A 5-level reading grid. It checks that an article does not overlook a dimension. It is a working model, not a law: levels are often worked on in parallel, and level 1 remains the base.

For each level: what the article contributes, and what depends on an action outside the article.

## Level 1 — Technical foundation (SEO)
Goal: search engines can read and understand the page.
- In the article: main keyword well placed (H1, intro, one H2, conclusion), clear heading hierarchy, meta title and description, clean slug, internal links, image alt text.
- Outside the article (recommend): site speed, mobile version, sitemap, pages without errors.

## Level 2 — Trust (E-E-A-T)
Goal: show real experience and expertise.
Note: E-E-A-T (experience, expertise, authoritativeness, trustworthiness) is a framework used in Google's quality guidelines. Do not present it as a direct "ranking factor".
- In the article: real evidence supplied by the user (product tests, photos, certifications, reviews), identified author, cited sources, last-updated date.
- Outside the article: collecting reviews, a solid "about" page, external mentions.
- Forbidden: inventing a test, a certification, or a review.

## Level 3 — Local relevance (local SEO)
Goal: answer the realities of the target market (country, laws, delivery times, customs).
- In the article: references to the legal framework and customs of the target country, real delivery times, local vocabulary. Verify any legal rule against an official source; otherwise label it [HYPOTHESIS].
- Outside the article: local business listing, location pages if relevant.
- This level applies only if the market is geographically defined.

## Level 4 — Visibility in AI answers (GEO, generative engine optimization)
Goal: become a source AI assistants can draw on.
Do not confuse with level 3: here "GEO" means generative engines.
- In the article: structured content (explicit headings, lists, tables), precise sourced data, clear definitions, an extractable summary, structured data.
- Outside the article: mentions and citations on other websites.
- Uncertain: nobody controls what AI systems cite. Promise no result.

## Level 5 — Direct answer (AEO, answer engine optimization)
Goal: provide the short answer to the reader's exact question.
- In the article: summary box at the top, a short direct answer under each H2, an FAQ section in question-then-answer format.
- Outside the article: nothing specific.

## Usage
In the outline, mark each level as "covered in the article", "to do outside the article", or "not relevant here". This gives the user a clear action list.

"Covered" means the article text contains at least one concrete element for that level (for example, real delivery times for level 3, a sourced table for level 4). If you only intend to mention the level, or the needed information is missing, write "partial" and say what the user must supply.
